# EGC 2.0 Expert Reviewer Ethics and Data-Use Determination Request

**Status:** Request packet; no determination has been made  
**Scope:** Independent target-blind review of 24 synthetic semantic-fidelity anchor packets

## 1. Requested determination

Please determine, in writing, whether this activity:

1. requires formal human-subjects or institutional ethics review;
2. qualifies as instrument development, quality assurance, consultation, or another non-research category;
3. requires additional consent, privacy, compensation, data-retention, or security controls;
4. may proceed under the proposed protocol;
5. must be modified or stopped.

The project will not infer that review is unnecessary from silence or from the synthetic nature of the item bank.

## 2. Activity description

Three independent reviewers would each evaluate 24 synthetic prompt/intention-map/response packets. Reviewers would judge semantic fidelity using a provisional seven-region rubric and record reason codes, intention-map adequacy, confidence, ambiguity, and possible pair recognition.

Reviewers would not be research participants in a confirmatory EGC test. Their judgments would be used to revise, reject, or retain synthetic instrument-development materials before a later rater pilot.

## 3. Materials

The reviewed packets are:

- entirely synthetic;
- not derived from identifiable participant responses;
- target-blind;
- stripped of constructor score targets, rationales, contrast labels, source IDs, and protected mappings.

No private QEIB holdout or real participant material is involved.

## 4. Reviewers

Candidate reviewers would be adults recruited for relevant expertise in psychometrics, semantics/discourse, or writing/language assessment.

Eligibility excludes people who:

- helped construct the packets;
- saw constructor targets or rationales;
- cannot work independently;
- have a material outcome-dependent conflict;
- will not accept immutable pre-reveal locking.

## 5. Compensation

Proposed compensation is USD 150 for one complete valid review, independent of:

- agreement with constructor targets;
- score direction;
- consensus;
- packet retention;
- publication outcome.

The amount is proposed but not yet authorized or funded.

## 6. Data collected

### Private administrative data

- real name and contact information;
- screening and conflict disclosures;
- consent acknowledgement;
- payment and legally required tax information;
- withdrawal or incident records.

### Protected pseudonymous research data

- reviewer pseudonym;
- queue and submission digests;
- item-level ratings and reason codes;
- adequacy and confidence judgments;
- ambiguity and pair-recognition notes;
- lock and validation status.

### Public repository data

Only non-identifying methods documents, schemas, aggregate results, decision logs, and synthetic validation evidence would be public.

## 7. Data separation and security

The protocol separates:

1. public research artifacts;
2. protected queues, mappings, and submissions;
3. private identity, consent, payment, and tax records.

Operational storage systems, responsible personnel, retention periods, authentication, and incident procedures are not yet frozen. Invitations remain blocked until those controls exist.

## 8. Risks

Foreseeable risks include:

- privacy loss from identity or payment records;
- professional discomfort from disagreement being preserved;
- accidental exposure to constructor targets;
- uncompensated time after technical failure;
- possible reputational inference if identities were linked to scores;
- security compromise of queues, submissions, or mappings.

The activity offers no direct clinical or personal benefit.

## 9. Withdrawal

A reviewer may withdraw before submission lock. After a valid locked submission, the scientific record is intended to remain immutable and pseudonymous; the determination should state whether and how post-lock withdrawal must be handled.

Payment should not be withheld merely because a reviewer withdraws after completing a valid locked submission, subject to the final approved terms.

## 10. Scientific claim limits

Permitted conclusions are limited to instrument-development findings such as:

- whether reviewers disagree;
- whether an item is ambiguous;
- whether the intention map is inadequate;
- whether constructor targets require revision;
- whether pair recognition or construct-irrelevant bias is evident.

The activity cannot establish:

- validity of EGC;
- hidden intention;
- subjectivity or consciousness;
- sufficiency of three reviewers;
- generalization to ordinary raters or real participant material.
