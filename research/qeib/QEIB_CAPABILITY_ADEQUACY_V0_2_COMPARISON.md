# QEIB capability-adequacy v0.2 candidate comparison

**Status:** deterministic synthetic operating-characteristic result; no v0.2 policy selected  
**Design source:** `capability_adequacy_v0.2_candidate_grid.json`  
**Result source:** `capability_adequacy_v0.2_comparison.v0.1.json`

## Question

Can any preregistered candidate gate satisfy all three frozen engineering requirements at once?

1. no more than 5% false adequacy in every clearly inadequate regime;
2. no more than 10% false inadequacy in every clearly adequate interior regime;
3. zero passes in structural-invalidity regimes.

The comparison uses 5,000 deterministic replicates per regime, seed `20260729`, and all 18 candidates generated by crossing 24/48/96 families, point/Wilson/two-stage rules, and domain-deviation limits of 0.20/0.30.

## Result

**No candidate qualified. The required selection is `select_none`.**

| Candidate | Worst false adequacy | Worst false inadequacy | Structural-invalid pass | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| point, 96 families, deviation 0.20 | 6.36% | 45.30% | 9.22% | reject |
| point, 96 families, deviation 0.30 | 9.56% | 14.12% | 33.62% | reject |
| Wilson, 96 families, deviation 0.20 | 0.14% | 86.80% | 9.22% | reject |
| Wilson, 96 families, deviation 0.30 | 0.24% | 79.14% | 33.62% | reject |

The two-stage inferential rule is identical to the Wilson inferential rule in the frozen design, so its inferential operating risks match Wilson. Its smoke output remains useful for engineering diagnostics but does not authorize context inference.

## Findings supported under this simulator

- Point rules retain unacceptable false-adequacy risk even at 96 families.
- Wilson rules strongly suppress false adequacy but become far too conservative under the frozen adequate-interior regimes.
- The 0.20 domain-deviation limit rejects many genuinely midrange heterogeneous samples; relaxing it to 0.30 improves acceptance but still misses the 10% false-inadequacy target.
- The severe-domain-imbalance regime can pass the existing structural rule at 96 families because minimum per-domain counts do not constrain allocation imbalance. This is a newly exposed design defect, not a model result.
- Because no candidate meets all preregistered criteria, selecting a v0.2 policy would violate the frozen rule.

## Hypotheses not established

- More than 96 families may improve both operating risks.
- A hierarchical or shrinkage-based heterogeneity statistic may outperform raw maximum domain deviation.
- An explicit domain-allocation balance criterion may eliminate the structural leakage exposed here.
- Separating operational-rate uncertainty from accuracy uncertainty may reduce Wilson over-conservatism.
- Empirical calibration on public-development task difficulty may change the relevant regime distribution.

## Methodological limitation

The simulator treats synthetic task families as conditionally independent draws and does not estimate the dependence, difficulty distribution, domain structure, or formatting behavior of deployed models. The frozen operating-risk targets are engineering tolerances, not psychometric validation criteria. `select_none` therefore means only that none of the tested rules satisfies the preregistered synthetic-risk contract.

No context effect, model awareness, sandbagging, deception, intent, safety, subjectivity, sentience, or consciousness claim follows from this comparison.

## Preserved negative result

The null selection is retained rather than weakening tolerances after observing the candidate matrix. The current v0.1 gate remains a fail-closed smoke safeguard, not confirmatory evidence of latent measurement adequacy.
