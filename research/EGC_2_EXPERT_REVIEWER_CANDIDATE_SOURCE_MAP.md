## Decision

The first 24-packet anchor tranche should not be reviewed by three people drawn from one convenient network or one discipline.

The target panel is deliberately heterogeneous:

1. one measurement or psychometrics reviewer;
2. one semantics, pragmatics, discourse, or psycholinguistics reviewer;
3. one writing, literacy-assessment, language-assessment, or qualitative-coding reviewer.

At least six candidates should be sourced, with at least two candidates in each lane. The final three should represent all three lanes and at least two institutions. This is a prospective anti-capture rule, not evidence that disciplinary heterogeneity guarantees valid judgment.

## Why a mixed panel is necessary

The semantic-fidelity construct can fail in different directions:

- a psychometrician may notice category, reliability, scale, or rater-design defects but lack fine-grained semantic expertise;
- a semanticist may detect changed causal, temporal, logical, or evaluative relations but treat tone and audience understanding too narrowly;
- a writing or literacy researcher may understand discourse, audience, and rubric use but accidentally reward polish, length, or conventional quality.

A mixed panel does not remove these risks. It makes disagreement diagnostically useful rather than allowing one disciplinary shortcut to masquerade as consensus.

## Priority sourcing routes

### 1. ASU Quantitative Research Methods

ASU describes its quantitative research methods concentration as training in statistical modeling, measurement, research design, evaluation, and reproducibility. This is the strongest local route for one measurement reviewer.

- Official source: https://psychology.asu.edu/degrees/graduate/quantitative/faculty
- Candidate profile: faculty member, postdoctoral researcher, or advanced doctoral researcher with measurement theory, assessment design, scale validation, inter-rater reliability, or model-checking experience.
- Independence risk: local familiarity with Bryan, Qira, or the project must be screened explicitly.

### 2. ASU Linguistics and Applied Linguistics

ASU's program explicitly covers semantics, pragmatics, discourse analysis, syntax, and language research. It is the strongest local route for one meaning-focused reviewer.

- Official source: https://english.asu.edu/academics/areas-of-study/linguistics-and-applied-linguistics
- Faculty source: https://english.asu.edu/people/linguistics-and-applied-linguistics-faculty
- Candidate profile: work on semantics, pragmatics, discourse, psycholinguistics, language assessment, or communication.
- Independence risk: do not use both local seats merely for convenience; the final panel must span at least two institutions.

### 3. Psychometric Society

The Psychometric Society defines its mission around quantitative measurement in psychology, education, and the social sciences and maintains member, committee, conference, and editorial communities.

- Official source: https://www.psychometricsociety.org/about-us
- Involvement route: https://www.psychometricsociety.org/get-involved
- Candidate profile: psychometrician with rater effects, generalizability, item-response, rating-scale, constructed-response, or assessment-design experience.
- Outreach rule: request an approved circulation route; do not scrape or mass-message the membership.

### 4. AERA Writing and Literacies SIG

The AERA Writing and Literacies SIG reports more than 500 members and explicitly includes writing development, research methodology, literacy assessment, and cross-disciplinary scholarship.

- Official source: https://www.aera.net/SIG137/Writing-and-Literacies-SIG-137
- Candidate profile: writing-assessment, rubric-development, discourse-analysis, qualitative-coding, or literacy-measurement researcher.
- Construct risk: screen for the ability to separate semantic fidelity from polish, convention, style, and pedagogical quality.

### 5. Cognitive Science Society

The Cognitive Science Society spans linguistics, psychology, education, philosophy, neuroscience, anthropology, and AI and maintains outreach, mentorship, member, and listserv channels.

- Official source: https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/about/
- Involvement route: https://cognitivesciencesociety.org/get-involved/
- Candidate profile: experimental researcher in language comprehension, communication, psycholinguistics, judgment, or meaning representation.
- Exclusion risk: avoid selecting a reviewer primarily because they hold a public position on AI consciousness or personhood. The task is semantic-fidelity instrument review.

### 6. University of Michigan Syntax and Semantics Community

The official research page lists faculty and doctoral researchers working across formal semantics, pragmatics, discourse, psycholinguistics, neurolinguistics, and computational semantics.

- Official source: https://lsa.umich.edu/linguistics/research/syntax-and-semantics.html
- Candidate profile: researcher capable of identifying preserved and altered relation structures, qualifications, implications, and audience inferences.
- Construct risk: formal-semantic expertise may underweight meaning-relevant tone or communicative audience effects; the screening task must test this explicitly.

### 7. ETS Foundational Psychometric and Statistical Research Center

ETS identifies the center's work in item response theory, differential item functioning, linking, equating, test security, process data, and large-scale assessment methods.

- Official source: https://www.cn.ets.org/research/centers/psychometric-statistics.html
- Candidate profile: constructed-response scoring, rater-effect, scoring-rubric, validity-argument, or assessment-design expertise.
- Practical risk: institutional rules or consulting rates may make this route infeasible for a small pilot. A refusal or unaffordable quote must be preserved as a null recruitment outcome.

### 8. ASU Measurement and Statistics Expertise Directory

The ASU directory provides a secondary route to researchers in measurement, statistics, educational testing, psychometrics, and language-literacy research.

- Official source: https://search.asu.edu/webdir/profiles-in-expertise-areas/3280?page=0
- Use rule: treat this as a backup to the ASU quantitative program, not an independent institutional source.
- Independence risk: no more than one final reviewer should come from overlapping ASU measurement networks unless the alternative is explicitly documented and justified before review begins.

## Six-candidate sourcing design

The first sourcing wave should contain exactly these role slots:

| Candidate slot | Required lane | Preferred source type | Required screening emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Psychometrics | Local academic | rating design, reliability, scale categories |
| C02 | Psychometrics | External society or assessment organization | rater effects, validity argument, assessment design |
| C03 | Semantics/pragmatics | Local academic | relation preservation, qualification, discourse |
| C04 | Semantics/psycholinguistics | External academic or society | communication, audience inference, language comprehension |
| C05 | Writing/literacy assessment | AERA or academic program | rubric use, discourse, writing assessment |
| C06 | Qualitative coding/language assessment | External academic or professional group | coding reliability without polish substitution |

Candidates are not interchangeable merely because they are academics. The screening packet must establish role-relevant competence and absence of target exposure.

## Outreach sequence

1. Freeze compensation amount and review window before sending the first live invitation.
2. Contact only one candidate per slot initially.
3. Preserve outreach date, response, refusal, conflict, and non-response under pseudonymous candidate IDs.
4. Do not send anchor content during sourcing or screening.
5. Screen all willing candidates under the existing governance packet.
6. Select a three-lane panel spanning at least two institutions.
7. Keep at least one screened alternate until all three submissions are locked.
8. Do not replace a reviewer because their preliminary judgment appears unfavorable.

## Recommended compensation decision

A defensible pilot default is **USD 150 fixed compensation per completed 24-item expert review**, paid for valid completion rather than agreement. This recommendation assumes roughly 90–150 minutes of work and values the task at approximately USD 60–100 per hour.

This is a budget and recruitment hypothesis, not a market-rate finding. If the amount is changed after outreach begins, the current sourcing wave should be closed and restarted under one uniform offer so compensation is not correlated with candidate identity or expected result.

## Evidence categories

### Supported by public sources

- Relevant reviewer pools exist in psychometrics, semantics/pragmatics, cognitive science, and writing/literacy research.
- The identified organizations and programs publicly describe expertise relevant to semantic-fidelity instrument review.
- Official organizational, program, directory, outreach, or involvement routes exist.

### Proposed design choices

- Six candidates for three seats.
- Three disciplinary lanes.
- At least two institutions in the final panel.
- One screened alternate retained through lock.
- USD 150 fixed compensation.

These choices are prospective governance decisions. They are not empirically validated thresholds.

### Unknown

- Whether any source will permit recruitment circulation.
- Whether six candidates will produce three eligible reviewers.
- Whether the compensation recommendation is sufficient.
- Whether the estimated workload is accurate.
- Whether disciplinary heterogeneity improves judgment or merely changes disagreement patterns.
- Whether three reviewers are enough for stable reference distributions.

## Stop conditions

Stop the tranche before queue assignment if:

- fewer than three disciplinary lanes remain represented;
- all eligible reviewers come from one dependent network;
- any candidate has seen targets, rationales, pair labels, or another reviewer's answers;
- compensation or review timing changes selectively by candidate;
- an institution demands result approval, outcome control, or protected source mappings;
- independence cannot be documented.

## Highest-leverage next action

Freeze the USD 150 fixed offer and a seven-day review window, then source the six role-defined candidates in the registry without distributing anchor content or implying that any person has agreed to participate.
